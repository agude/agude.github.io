I think most software engineers are starting to wonder about their and their
field's future with the rise of LLM coding. We prided ourselves on our
_technical ability_---that's what allowed me to pick the [correct side of the
ML/DS split][split]---but in less then a year machines have completely
surpassed us. What do we bring to the table if not that?

<!-- Maybe the engineeriness article is better -->

[split]: {% post_url 2021-05-31-the_data_science_split %}

I've been having a version of this conversation with friends and colleagues
pretty regularly now:

> **Me**: We aren't really hiring junior engineers anymore. And we just laid
> off a bunch of them. Kept most of the senior and staff engineers though.
>
> **Friend**: Yeah... But where are the next senior and staff engineers going
> to come from if juniors don't learn on the job?
>
> **Me**: I guess we think that's not our problem. But... did they need
> another generation of apprentices once the [Jacquard loom][jl_wiki] was
> invented?

it collapses dramatically. At some point it will not be possible to raise
the bar, stay above the wave, and it is plausible that nothing biological
will be able to give valuable insights to the silicon. The dragon will know
what to do. But we live now, not in three years.
